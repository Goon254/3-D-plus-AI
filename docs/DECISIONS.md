# Decision log

One line per decision: what + why. Newest at the bottom.

1. **Core license Apache-2.0** (the repo already had an Apache LICENSE); Blender adapter and TTS worker GPL-3.0-or-later in their own folders with their own LICENSE files.
2. **Blender via the PyPI `bpy` 5.3 wheel** in a child process speaking newline-delimited JSON over stdio — installs with pip, no apt/tarball, keeps GPL code out of the core process.
3. **Rollback = snapshot .blend before each mutating batch** (`undo_token` is its path) because Blender's undo stack is unreliable in background mode; identical behaviour headless and live.
4. **Stable ids = object names + `animatica_id` custom property**, so identity survives artist renames.
5. **Rotations in degrees, colours as `#RRGGBB` sRGB** in the protocol — what LLMs and artists write naturally; the adapter converts to radians / linear.
6. **Object-level rig (empties + parenting) instead of an armature for the hero** tonight: robust, readable in the outliner, animatable with batched keys; `create_armature` + `bind_mesh_to_armature` exist in the protocol for later.
7. **Mouth = small mesh with three shape keys (open / wide / round)**; Rhubarb's 9 visemes map to weights of those three. Cheap, readable, stylised.
8. **Parameters over strokes for the LLM**: agents ask the model for plan/performance/camera *parameters*; procedural recipes turn them into ops. LLM output is validated; unusable answers fall back to the scripted answer and the fallback is logged.
9. **Scripted offline provider = keyword parser + recipes**, deterministic per prompt (crc32 seeds, no Python `hash`).
10. **Animator samples composite curves every 2 frames** (pose curves with easing + spring follow-through + bob/breathing layers) into one batched keyframe op per channel; lip-sync keys are exact.
11. **Multi-shot = one camera per shot + timeline markers bound to cameras**, so a single render pass produces the cut and an artist sees the cuts in Blender's timeline.
12. **Critic previews: Workbench for per-batch verify, Cycles 12 spp for the per-shot exposure check.** Research recommends Workbench previews; we follow that for batch verification, but Workbench can't judge lighting/exposure, so the critic's 4 per-shot frames use low-sample Cycles (~2.5 s each). Switch with `--preview-engine workbench`.
13. **Final render: Cycles CPU 16 spp + OIDN + adaptive sampling + persistent data, 960×540.** EEVEE needs a GPU (software GL took 75 s for one frame).
14. **TTS: Kokoro-82M (kokoro-onnx) default, Piper fallback, both in a separate GPL worker process** (research report 03: Piper is GPL since 2025 and espeak-ng sits under Kokoro's phonemizer). Replaced the first implementation, which called Piper directly — logged per PLAN update rule 3.
15. **Piper voices restricted to CC0 (kathleen) / CC BY 4.0 (LibriTTS) datasets**; lessac (research-only) and ryan (CC BY-NC-SA) were downloaded, rejected, and deleted.
16. **Music: numpy procedural pads/arp/bass + wind ambience** (research: ADOPT for ambient beds and the offline path). FluidSynth + GeneralUser GS is the planned upgrade; apt had no fluidsynth candidate here.
17. **Dialogue ducking done in numpy** (-9 dB envelope with 150 ms smoothing) and final mux in ffmpeg; the mix is also placed on the .blend's VSE so artists hear it.
18. **Two-shot close-up for dialogue**: the "speak" close-up frames the hero's face *and* the prop it addresses (no rule-of-thirds offset), because the emotional beat is the relationship.
19. **Sun disc sits lower (4°) than the key light (12°)** — classic cheat so the sun reads on the horizon while the light still rakes the set.
20. **Quality presets**: `ci` (Workbench 192×108), `preview` (Workbench 480×270), `draft` (Cycles 640×360 8 spp), `final` (Cycles 960×540 16 spp), `high` (Cycles 1280×720 48 spp).
21. **Godot 4.5.1 from the official GitHub release** (research mentions 4.7 as current; 4.5.1 was the first URL that resolved and it runs headless fine). The runner is a `SceneTree` script (`-s runner.gd -- batch.json result.json`): transactional because the scene/project are only written by `save_scene` and at the end.
22. **Games track scripts come from parameterised GDScript templates** (player, collectible, level controller); the LLM/scripted designer picks physics feel, theme and counts; the level builder checks reachability against jump physics before building.
23. **Playtester = a bot inside the game** (`--playtest`): steers to the nearest collectible, jumps at walls/gaps/higher targets; runs headless at `--fixed-fps 60` (a 10 s run takes ~0.3 s). Screenshots via `xvfb-run` + `--rendering-driver opengl3` because `--headless` can't render.
24. **No game export tonight**: export templates are ~1 GB; the deliverable is a runnable Godot project (`godot --path …`).
25. **`tts` extra is Kokoro, Piper is opt-in** (review 2026-11-15T08:45Z): `pip install animatica[tts]` now installs `kokoro-onnx` + `soundfile` (MIT/BSD). The GPL `piper-tts` fallback moved to a separate `tts-piper` extra so a default install of the Apache-2.0 core never pulls GPL code; Piper still runs only as a subprocess of the GPL TTS worker. `THIRD_PARTY.md` now also lists Godot, Xvfb, the MCP and Anthropic SDKs, ONNX Runtime and soundfile.
26. **Review-queue response (09:00):** robot mouth enlarged and the dialogue shot reframed as a true head-and-shoulders close-up; Critic gains `face_too_small`/`face_cut` checks during dialogue; music high-passed at 120 Hz with pads above the voice; ground/set materials get procedural grime and an aerial-perspective fade to the horizon colour; sun gets a radial-glow halo (hidden in Workbench, which can't do transparency); Workbench uses FLAT lighting.
27. **Story arcs vary**: the scripted Director picks an opening (enter | idle) and ending (settle | celebrate | exit) from the prompt; the prop can bloom on cue. Still template-driven; an LLM plan replaces this when a key is present.
28. **Edit hand-off as MLT XML** (`edit.mlt`, research 05): per-shot clips on V1, dialogue on A1 at exact frames, music on A2 — opens in Shotcut/Kdenlive. We write the XML ourselves (no MLT dependency); `melt` rendering of it is a later step.
29. **Wheels roll by distance, capped at 35°/frame** (review 10:36): each robot cog is an animatable wheel pivot with a spoke and an off-centre bolt; the Animator keys rotation = arc length / radius. Without motion blur faster spins strobe (wagon-wheel effect), so the per-frame step is capped — a cartoon cheat that still reads as driving.
30. **Ground is fully rough with low specular** (`MaterialSpec.specular`, optional): the grazing-angle sheen made a bright band at the horizon that detached distant scrap heaps from the ground. Heaps are now smooth, displaced mounds instead of 20-face icosahedra.
31. **Critters hop, they don't glide**: the root path is quantised into ~0.42 s hop cycles (travel while airborne, still on contact, landing squash). Positions at cycle boundaries match the plan, so cameras and the critic stay valid (≤ one cycle of lag). Every frame is keyed for critters, since a hop is only ~10 frames.
32. **An exit leaves the frame; the camera holds** (review 12:36): the exit shot is 6.5 s. The hero turns, looks back, then accelerates for 1 s and cruises at 2.2 m/s × scale out of frame. The camera frames the departure point, not the whole path, so the last ~1 s is the empty set with the prop. The Critic applies framing rules only up to the departure frame. After that it requires the hero to be out of frame on the last sample (`exit_in_frame`, fix: `exit_speed_mul`). Longer critter hops arc higher (up to 2×).
33. **Critter body rests on the floor**: the Animator's scale keys make the critter body a 0.36-radius sphere, not the asset's 0.85-scaled one. The squash anchor used the wrong half-height (0.306), so the critter sat 3.4 cm inside the floor and the critic spent iterations on it. `body_half` is now 0.36 and `body_base_z` 0.365, so the look is unchanged and contact is correct at the source.
34. **Night previews use a viewport-only grade** (`set_world.viewport_tint`): Workbench FLAT shading ignores lights, so night films looked like day in `--quality preview`. The grade multiplies `Material.diffuse_color`, which only Workbench and the viewport use. The ungraded colour is stored on the material, so the grade can be removed again. Emissive materials show their emission colour. Cycles/EEVEE output is unchanged.
35. **Ground detail is shader grit + scattered pebbles, not image textures**: a second fine noise (×colour, plus a bump) and 36 small stones give scale cues with no downloaded assets and no license surface. CC0 Poly Haven/ambientCG textures remain a later upgrade. Scrap heaps break their silhouette with procedural junk on the crest.
36. **Bones where they show, objects where they don't** (review 11-16 17:55): four-legged critters get a real armature (`hero_rig`: 3-bone tail chain, two-bone legs). The plume is smooth-skinned so it *bends* with overlapping action, and the legs tuck/reach through the hop. Body and head stay object-level pivots (spheres don't deform, and those curves are tested and tuned), and so does the robot (rigid boxes on hinges look identical with bones). Skin weights are computed by distance to the bone segments, not Blender's bone heat, which can fail on primitives. Binds keep each part's local transform, so scene-model replay and artist-edit detection are unaffected.
37. **Practical lights escape their housings via `cast_shadow: false`** (review 11-16 17:55): the lantern's point light sat inside an opaque emissive glass cylinder, which blocked every shadow ray, so the "glowing lantern" only lit the snow through the glass's own emission. Objects can now opt out of shadow casting (`_Placed.cast_shadow`, Blender `visible_shadow`); the lantern glass and crystal gems use it, and the night lantern is ~77 W. Cheaper and more controllable than a transmissive glass shader.
38. **Prop slivers in a dialogue single are cropped by raising the bottom edge, not by tilting** (critic `prop_sliver`, fix `lift_frac`): tilting the whole frame up sank the fox's eyes to the lower half under a band of empty sky. Raising only the bottom edge keeps the ear-tip headroom, so the face gets bigger and the eyes stay high. The addressee stays present through the medium before and the glance (warm lantern light on the face from below also motivates it). An insert shot of the prop remains a possible upgrade.
39. **Snow gets few, large half-buried rocks; fur gets a Principled sheen** (`MaterialSpec.sheen`): many small dark stones on bright snow read as ash; a coat with no sheen reads as plastic. Both are shader/primitive changes with no new assets.
40. **Savannah characters are sculpted procedurally with metaballs, not stacked primitives, base meshes or TripoSR** (owner feedback on walk tests, 2026-11-16). Options compared:
    * *Skin modifier + Subdivision along the skeleton* (`create_skin_mesh`): it works, but branch hulls where the legs leave the spine grew flat fins, and cross-sections read boxy. Kept for thin tubes (tails).
    * *Metaballs* (`create_metaball_mesh`): overlapping ellipsoids and tapering ball runs on the anatomy graph (spine with belly, shoulder/haunch muscle masses, thigh-knee-cannon-foot, neck into cranium-head-nose, trunk) melt into one smooth body, converted to a mesh and skinned with bone heat. **Best in renders** (before/after reel); chosen.
    * *CC0/CC-BY base meshes*: no confirmed CC0 3D set covers the cast. Quaternius's CC0 animated packs are farm and forest animals (a horse could base a zebra and nothing else), Kenney's savannah animals are 2D, and the rigged savannah packs found were paid or of unconfirmed licence. One-off models would also clash in style, and each would need re-rigging to our gait skeleton. Rejected for now.
    * *TripoSR* (MIT code and weights, ~6 GB VRAM): structurally a poor fit here. It needs a single input image, and we have no text-to-image model, so the only licence-clean inputs are our own renders or CC0/PD photos (no CC0 side-view zebra photo was found on Commons; CC-BY-SA inputs would push share-alike obligations onto the output). The output is a static, unrigged marching-cubes mesh with baked colours, which we would still have to re-topologise and rig. A hands-on test (input: a render of our own zebra, PyMCubes shim in place of torchmcubes, which needs MSVC/CUDA to build) was set up but **not run**: the dependency install was stopped by the host for low memory. Revisit if a text-to-image step or a better image-to-3D model (e.g. SF3D) becomes available.
    The gait system and rig are unchanged by the switch. The hind-leg IK pole-angle fix (+90 deg for poles behind the knee) came out of this work.

41. **The score is written as MIDI and rendered with SoundFont instruments** (CHALLENGE-SAVANNAH §5, research 03 ADOPT): a Composer (`animatica.audio.composer`) arranges one cue per scene from a cue sheet (`films/savannah/05_cue_sheet.json`: key, tempo, sections of mood + mode + leitmotifs). Each character owns a melody *and* a timbre (main theme kalimba, Tembo marimba + tuba, Zuri piccolo, Kiki pizzicato, Neema flute, Bibi Kobe hummed voices); motifs are stored as scale degrees, so the same tune turns minor at the low point and major on the hero's choice. Moods choose pad, bass, ostinato (kalimba/mbira 3-against-2, interlocking marimba) and hand-percussion patterns. A tiny in-house SMF writer/reader (`audio/midi.py`) replaces `mido`/`pretty_midi` (no new dependency). FluidSynth is called as a CLI subprocess (LGPL stays out of process); the SoundFont is GeneralUser GS 2.0.3, downloaded at setup from the author's GitHub repo pinned to a commit and checked by SHA-256 (the license asks not to hot-link the author's *website* downloads; the 32 MB file stays out of git, over the 25 MB limit anyway). Without FluidSynth a numpy additive synth plays the same notes, so CI and the pipeline never break. Melodies are original; the East/Southern African colour comes from instrumentation and rhythm (lamellophone cross-rhythm, interlocking marimba, shakers, congas), not from any existing song.
42. **A numpy SoundFont player sits between FluidSynth and the additive synth** (owner's Windows laptop, 2026-11-16). Running a downloaded FluidSynth binary was not approved there, so the Composer (#41) fell back to its additive synth, which has no real instruments. `animatica.audio.soundfont` parses SF2/SF3 (presets → instruments → zones, root key and tuning, looped sustains with release, the volume envelope, attenuation, pan; SF3 Ogg samples via `soundfile`) and plays the same arranged tracks. `composer.renderer()` order: `fluidsynth` (binary + font) → `soundfont` (any cached font; FluidR3Mono_GM, MIT, fetched by `setup_assets.sh`) → `numpy`. Pitch-checked at A4 = 440 Hz across instruments; all 14 GM programs the score uses exist in the font. The 5:22 score renders in ~12 s. Not implemented: modulators, filter/modulation envelopes and LFOs, so timbres are a little plainer than FluidSynth's.


43. **The film is produced scene by scene from machine-readable specs** (CHALLENGE-SAVANNAH §6, 2026-11-17). `films/savannah/07_scenes.json` holds one spec per scene (layout, cast marks/paths + gaits, lines with expression beats, reactions, SFX, shot list), written from `04_screenplay.md` by the writer (this Claude session) rather than parsed from prose, because blocking and coverage need numbers the screenplay doesn't carry. Lines are timed from the real TTS durations (never overlapping), cuts land 0.35 s before the first word with a 1.2 s minimum shot, and cameras are framed on the head positions measured on the posed rig (shot size → frame height; lens → distance), always within 70° of the scene's camera side so screen direction holds across cuts. Each scene's Composer cue is re-arranged to the scene's real length (the arrangement re-fits whole bars), so music changes on scene cuts by construction. Scene mixes are not loudness-normalised individually in the film edit; one −14 LUFS pass on the whole film keeps the scenes' relative dynamics (a scene preview is normalised on its own). `scripts/savannah_scene.py` is the driver; the pure parts (`animatica.production.scene`, `.sound`) are unit-tested.
44. **Cast voices are blends of stock Kokoro voices with small pitch shifts, natural delivery, and outdoor placement; Chatterbox stays a trial** (owner: "some voices sound robotic", 2026-11-16).
    * *Casting.* The first casting reached its characters with big post-TTS shifts: Kiki +5.6 st, Tembo +4.8, Bibi Kobe −4.7. Shifting that far moves the formants with the pitch (a sped-up-tape sound). A survey of all 28 English Kokoro voices (median F0 and spectral centroid) picked a native voice near each target. Each character then blends two voices' style vectors (`"af_bella:0.6+af_jessica:0.4"`, done in the TTS worker), and any shift is capped at ±2 st (a test enforces this on `06_voices.json`). Blending mixes existing stock voices; it does not clone anyone.
    * *Delivery* (`audio/delivery.py`). Each line has a mood pace (taken from the scene's expression beat or a parenthetical word), a ±3 % variation per line, and punctuation pauses: question 0.38 s, ellipsis 0.52 s, full stop 0.34 s, exclamation 0.26 s, all scaled by the mood. A soft synthetic breath goes after sentences of 7+ words. Emphasis comes from `*word*` markup or CAPS: primary stress plus a length mark in the phonemes, and a +2.5 dB lift timed by rendering the line's prefix.
      * We first tried speaking each phrase in a separate call. That made Kokoro over-release the final consonants ("panic." came back as "panicked" in a Whisper check), so the line is spoken in one call and the model's own gaps at the phrase boundaries are widened afterwards.
    * *Placement* (`audio/voice_fx.py`). Windowed-sinc resampling replaces the old linear interpolation, which put images of the 24 kHz voice above 12 kHz at −57 dB; the sinc version leaves them at −97 dB. The TTS sample rate is now Kokoro's native 24 kHz. Each line then gets:
      * a gentle EQ;
      * 2:1 compression;
      * an outdoor impulse response: a ground bounce plus 3 dark reflections at 26–72 ms and a 120 ms air tail, with no room reverb;
      * −56 dBFS room tone.
    * *Engine trial.* Research 03 lists two expressive TRIAL engines: Chatterbox (MIT code and weights) and Kyutai Pocket TTS (gated weights, some of its voices CC-BY-NC). Chatterbox was A/B'd against Kokoro on 3 lines in its own CPU venv (`scripts/tts_trial_worker.py`; torch 2.6 CUDA builds don't support the RTX 50 series). It was steered by each character's own Kokoro take of another line, so the voices match and no person's voice is used.
      * Results: word error rate 0.04 for Chatterbox against 0.00 for Kokoro. Chatterbox is much slower: it needs torch and runs on CPU here (not timed per line), while Kokoro takes well under a second per line.
      * The owner judges expressiveness by ear (`examples/showcase/savannah-sound/voices.m4a`). Kokoro stays the default until then.

45. **Chatterbox is the default voice engine, conditioned on the approved Kokoro blends, on the GPU; Kokoro is the fallback** (owner approved the voices, 2026-11-17; supersedes the "Kokoro stays the default" part of #44).
    * *Conditioning.* Each character's voice is steered by that character's own Kokoro take of a neutral `ref_text` line in `06_voices.json` (not a screenplay line, so Chatterbox copies the voice, not a line's delivery). That take uses the approved blend, rate and pitch shift, so the ±2 semitone cap (#44) still holds and no person's voice is used.
    * *GPU.* torch 2.6 has no Blackwell (sm_120) kernels, so the Chatterbox venv runs torch/torchaudio 2.7.1+cu128 installed with `--no-deps` over chatterbox-tts 0.1.7's `torch==2.6.0` pin (it runs unchanged). On the RTX 5060: ~25 s model load, then a few seconds per line; one load per scene (`tts.synthesize_batch`). `ANIMATICA_CHATTERBOX_DEVICE=cpu` forces CPU.
    * *Speech-recognition check.* The worker (`audio/chatterbox_worker.py`, own venv; the core never imports torch) transcribes every take with faster-whisper `base.en`. Above 0.10 word error rate it regenerates with the next seed (3 tries) and keeps the best. Above 0.25, or if the take's median pitch drifts more than 5 st from the conditioning take, or the worker fails, the line is spoken by Kokoro and the report says why (`fallback`). Emotional rises within that range are kept: they are prosody, not a pitch shift.
    * *Delivery.* Chatterbox's own prosody is kept, and the delivery pass from #44 runs on top: emphasis lifts are timed by Whisper's word timestamps (Chatterbox has no phoneme input), the mood's pace by WSOLA (the conditioning take already carries the character's base rate), punctuation pauses and breaths by the same gap-widening as Kokoro. Mood also sets Chatterbox's `exaggeration` (sad 0.4 … scared 0.8; cfg 0.4 above 0.7).
    * *Names and compounds.* The check primes Whisper with the line's proper names (`tts.asr_prompt`) and joins hyphenated compounds before scoring: SC 2's "Neema, you lovely lamp-post" first fell back to Kokoro at WER 0.33 because Whisper wrote "Nima" and "lamppost" for a correct take.
    * *Line cache.* Every take is content-addressed (`~/.cache/animatica/lines/`, key = text + voice fields + engine + a hash of the TTS, delivery, voice-fx and worker code), so re-running a scene only speaks changed lines and editing the code invalidates stale takes. Worker failures are not cached.

46. **Eyes sit in sockets: the eyeball is sunk 65 % into the real skin, lids wrap it, brows are laid on the skin, and the skull is rigid** (owner: "eyes popping out of their sockets", worst on the zebra; 2026-11-17).
    * *Why they popped.* The eyes were whole spheres at a nominal offset on an idealised head ellipsoid; the sculpted (metaball) heads swell differently, so the balls sat mostly outside the skin. And the melted head's skin was bone-heat weighted across neck and head bones while the eyes rode the head bone alone, so the skin slid around them when the neck moved.
    * *Socket.* The socket axis is the head ellipsoid's surface normal at the eye. `snap_to_surface` casts onto the real (sculpted) skin along it and sinks the centre 0.3 radii under the surface: (1 + 0.3) / 2 = 65 % buried. It now takes `carry` so the iris, glint and lids move with the ball. The iris looks along the socket axis turned up to 42° toward the face's forward (a stylised, readable gaze even on the zebra's side-set eyes).
    * *Lids* (`creatures/eyes.py`): thin spherical-cap shells (1.10–1.18 eye radii, so they never cut the ball) opening from the eye's up and down axes, in a skin tone a shade darker than the coat. Shape keys: `blink` (idle blinks now close the lids instead of squashing the eyeball), `droop` and `wide` (driven by the expression: sad/determined heavy, surprised/scared wide).
    * *Brows* are snapped with their centre just under the skin, plus a Shrinkwrap (keep outside, evaluated every frame on the deformed skin): the underside lies on the skin. Raised brows also sink along the brow ridge, so they slide on it instead of lifting off.
    * *Rigid skull.* Auto-bound bodies get a `rigid` region: inside the head ellipsoid (×1.24) the skin follows the head bone alone, blending back to the bone-heat weights over the outer 40 %. Everything on the head bone (eyes, lids, brows, mouth, muzzle) then moves exactly with the skin.
    * *Critic.* `inspect` gained `seating` (per frame on the evaluated meshes: an eye's embed share; a brow's or lid's gap to the skin), and `creatures/qa.py` turns it into `eye_socket` (embed 50–80 %, drift ≤ 10 % of the diameter), `brow_float` and `lid_float` issues. Measured on all five walking: embed 64.5–64.9 %, zero drift, brows ≤ +0.06 of their size, lids sunk in the skin (`scripts/creature_eye_test.py`). A Blender test shows the old stuck-on eye fails the check and the socketed one passes.
47. **Coat patterns are pinned to the rest pose with Blender's `rest_position` attribute** (owner: stripes, patches and scales slide as the rig deforms; 2026-11-17).
    * Procedural textures sampled `Texture Coordinate > Object`, which on a skinned mesh is the *deformed* position, so the pattern stayed put in space while the skin moved through it.
    * `set_material` / `set_material_by_bone` now detect a mesh with an Armature modifier, turn on `Object.add_rest_position_attribute`, and build the material with an Attribute node (`rest_position`) in place of Object coordinates for every pattern, grime, wrinkle, fur and grain texture. A material that has gone "rest" stays rest when another object reuses its name (the elephant's ears rebuilt `elephant_coat` with Object coordinates), and every user of it gets the attribute (for a rigid part it equals its Object coordinates).
    * Chosen over baked UVs: no unwrap of metaball meshes, no texture images, and the procedural looks stay exactly as tuned.
    * *Critic.* `inspect.swim` reads each deforming mesh's texture coordinates on a fixed vertex sample and reports p95/max drift from the first frame; `texture_swim` fails above 2 mm. Before: the elephant's skin wrinkles slid 142 mm (p95) over a walk. After: 0.0 mm on all five. A Blender test bends a striped tube both ways.
48. **Meerkat v3: slim and long-legged, dorsal-only bands, painted eye patches** (owner: "walks like a striped hamster", "eye patches like black sunglasses"; 2026-11-17). Torso 0.122 × 0.27 × 0.13 m → 0.084 × 0.235 × 0.09 m (length/width 2.2 → 2.8), body 0.105 → 0.128 m up on longer legs, a narrower head (0.06 m) with a long tapering snout. A first try at 3.3× with short legs read as a sausage dog; 2.8× with daylight under the belly reads as a mongoose. The back bands are fainter (#9E774A on #B08452), thinner and more irregular, and only on the top of the back: new `MaterialSpec.pattern_above` fades markings in above an object-space height (rest coordinates, so they stay put when she stands up). The two black patch spheres that enclosed the eyes are gone; new `MaterialSpec.marks` paints soft ellipsoids in object space, and `Species.eye_patch` puts a teardrop round each eye, reaching back and down, so the socketed eyes and their dark lids sit visibly inside a subtle patch. A material shared by name now gets one spec everywhere (the ears had rebuilt `meerkat_coat` without the marks).
49. **Acting is a layer of body-space rotations composed over the locomotion keys** (review 11-17 01:30: "the gag isn't in the picture"). `animatica.creatures.gesture` describes every acting beat as a rotation about an axis in the creature's body frame (turn about the world vertical, pitch nose-down, swing an ear outward), and converts it into each bone's own rest frame with Blender's roll-0 bone matrix (`B^T R B`), so one description drives every species' rig. The layer is composed *outside* the gait keys (`posed = R_gesture · rest · R_gait`), so a head that turns keeps its walk nod and a drinking trunk keeps a little sway. Alternatives rejected: Blender Damped Track / IK constraints with keyed influence (needs new adapter ops, the constraint fights the per-frame gait keys, and the critic could no longer predict poses in pure Python), and hand-posed actions per species (doesn't scale to 10 scenes × 6 characters).
    * **Eyelines are automatic**: listeners turn toward whoever is speaking from just before the first word to 0.5 s after the last; a speaker turns 45 % of the way toward the line's addressee (`"to"`), so faces stay cheated to the lens. The angle and its on/off weight go through two cascaded low-passes (τ 0.11 s): a 70° turn takes ≈ 0.5 s and eases in and out (a test bounds the per-frame speed). Turns are capped at 70°; the neck chain takes 55 %, the head the rest. The axis is the *world* vertical expressed in the body frame, so the upright meerkat turns her head, not her chin.
    * **Gestures** (`"gestures"` in the scene spec): `trunk_drink` (dip → suck with a wobble → lift and curl), `trunk_spray` (wind-up and aim at a cast member → recoil jolt), `head_duck`, `ears_droop`, `head_shake`, `nod`, `look_at`. Long holds (Neema ducking through the whole scene) don't stretch the scene's length.
    * **The spray is keyed geometry, not a particle system**: ~70 small ellipsoid drops leave the measured trunk tip (inspected on the posed rig) on ballistic arcs that land on the target's head, stretched along their velocity as a cheap motion streak, then splatter and drip to the ground; each is scaled to 0 before launch and on landing. Deterministic, renders identically in every engine and on every device, and needs no new adapter op.
50. **Cameras swing around animals that stand in the way** (found while staging the SC 2 gag: Zuri's flank filled Tembo's close-up). `frame_shot` takes the other cast members as occluders (body and head, as vertical cylinders measured on the plan) and tests the sight line with a frustum-shaped margin: anything within the middle 70 % of the frame width at its distance, rising above the frame's bottom edge there, counts.
