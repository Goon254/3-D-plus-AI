New problems the sheet shows:
* **The mane is a flat striped board.** In the side and back views it stands like a fin or plank behind the neck,
  with a visible step where it meets the crest near the poll. A zebra mane is short and stiff and follows the curve
  of the neck from the poll to the withers, standing about 10–15 % of neck depth.
* **The hooves are hockey pucks.** They are flat black cylinders, a little wider than the leg, and the striped leg
  tube sits on top of them, so they read as separate toy parts at 2×. They need a tapered hoof with a dark coronet
  band blending into the pastern.
* **Pony silhouette.** The head is small and long-faced, the legs long and thin, and the belly level. For a
  kids'-film zebra (a young filly), give her a bigger head (`head` 1.15–1.2), shorter legs and a rounder barrel.
* **Face**: heavy grey upper lids make her look sleepy, the eyes are small for the head, and the mouth still hides
  under the muzzle knob (happy 2.4 %).
* The stripe width is very even. Real zebras have broad flank stripes that narrow toward the face and legs, and
  shadow stripes on the rump. That is polish, not a blocker.

## Code notes (small)

* Kiki's paw darkening is a `marks` ellipsoid at a hard-coded `z = -0.118`, which repeats `body_z`. If `body_z`
  changes (a morph, or v5), the shading slides up or down the leg silently. Derive it from `body_z` in `build`, or
  add a test that ties them together.
* `materials:flat_black` only checks region base colours, not `marks` colours. A near-black paw mark would pass the
  check that exists to catch black boots. Extend `measure.materials` to marks on body regions.

## Addendum: Bibi Kobe v2 (`580c9a5`, DECISIONS #57; landed while this review was being pushed)

Verified on `design/bibi_kobe/v2/sheet.jpg`:

Still keeping her from approval on first sight:
* **The rays are stair-stepped**: jagged, pixel-like edges on every plate at full sheet size. That comes from

  pattern in rest-pose shell space (or a much denser vertex grid) so the edges are smooth.

* The head is tiny next to the shell for a kids'-film elder (`head` 1.15–1.25).
