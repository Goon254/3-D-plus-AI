## What I saw (walk tests)

* **Elephant (Tembo):** this is the most appealing of the five. It reads instantly as an elephant calf: domed head, big
  ears, trunk swinging, pillar legs, and it rides over near-straight legs. But it has no mouth, and the eyes are
  side-mounted "googly" spheres. The knee capsules show a visible seam.
* **Giraffe (Neema):** it reads as a giraffe from the patches and the neck, but it is a stick figure. The legs are
  pencil-thin with knobby joint balls, the head is tiny (ossicones barely visible) and there is no mane. The legs need
  more mass at the top (forearm and gaskin).
* **Zebra (Zuri):** the stripes and the stripe mane read well. The muzzle is a black ball (a clown nose), and the
  stripes run straight down the legs instead of becoming fine horizontal leg bands.
* **Meerkat (Kiki):** **does not read as a meerkat.** In profile it is a long, low, tiger-striped weasel or thylacine.
  The stripes are far too bold, and it lacks the meerkat's key marks: dark eye patches, dark ears, a dark tail tip and
  the upright sentinel posture. It is also tiny in frame.
* **Tortoise (Bibi Kobe):** reads as a spotted egg sitting on a plate. The shell has no scutes, there is a visible gap
  between the dome and the plastron disc, and the legs are smooth sausages with dark "shoes". A leopard tortoise shell
  has raised scutes, each with dark rays and blotches.
* **Set:** a flat sand plane with pebbles and a pale sky. That is fine for gait tests, but nothing of the savannah look
  exists yet: grass, acacias, termite mounds, the watering hole, mountains and the day cycle.

## Built this session: the Composer (CHALLENGE §5, queue "Audio to 9")

* `animatica.audio.composer`: one cue per scene from a cue sheet (`films/savannah/05_cue_sheet.json`, taken from the
  screenplay's intent and music notes). Each cue has a key, a tempo and sections (mood + mode + leitmotifs). Mood
  styles choose the pad, bass, ostinato (kalimba with a 3-against-2 mbira figure, interlocking marimba, harp arpeggios,
  celesta twinkles) and hand-percussion patterns. Cues end on a cadence (V→I, harmonic-minor V in minor keys), and the
  bpm is nudged so a whole number of bars lands exactly on the scene length.
* Six original leitmotifs, each with its own timbre (main: kalimba; Tembo: marimba + tuba; Zuri: piccolo; Kiki:
  pizzicato; Neema: flute; Bibi Kobe: hummed voices). They are stored as scale degrees, so Tembo's tune is minor at the
  low point (sc07) and major on his choice.
* `audio/midi.py`: a dependency-free SMF writer and reader. Every cue is also a `.mid` file that an artist can open
  in MuseScore or a DAW.
* Rendering uses FluidSynth (a CLI subprocess) with GeneralUser GS. A numpy fallback plays the same notes.
* Showcase `examples/showcase/savannah-score/`: `score.m4a` (5:22, 5.3 MB), ten `.mid` files, `score.json` and a
  spectrogram. Measured: −18.9 LUFS integrated before the dialogue mix, no rumble (< 60 Hz energy < 1 %), all 10 cue
  fingerprints and instrument sets distinct, keys D/F/A/G/E/B/D/E♭/C/D, tempos 55–120 bpm. I found and fixed a
  near-silent second at the end of sc09: its comedy button chord decayed to −44 dB before sc10, so a string hold now
  carries every cadence that has no pad.
* **Honest limit:** this machine has no speakers. I verified the score analytically (MIDI content per section, levels
  per second, spectrogram, the mode changes). Nobody has listened to it by ear yet. The owner, or the laptop session,
  should listen to `score.m4a` before the film mix.
* Tests (`tests/test_composer.py`, 9): MIDI round trip, cues land on scene lengths, no two scenes share a cue, every
  character's motif appears in ≥ 2 cues with a distinct timbre, Tembo minor→major, drums follow the mood, the build
  crescendos, cadences land on the tonic, numpy render, FluidSynth render (louder journey than low point, no rumble).
