# African bush elephant (calf) — Loxodonta africana

## Elephant proportions (calf, stylized from real ratios)
* Newborn calves stand about **1.0 m** at the shoulder (adults 3–4 m). Our calf, "about a year old", is
  1.2 m at the shoulder. [San Diego Zoo; SeaWorld]
* Body: barrel-shaped, deep chest, short back. Body length ≈ 1.15 × shoulder height. Legs are pillars:
  near-vertical columns with a straight elbow and knee at stance. Leg length ≈ 0.55 × shoulder height.
* Head: large and domed, ≈ 0.35 × shoulder height. The trunk reaches nearly to the ground (≈ 0.75 × head
  height to the floor in a calf).
* Ears: African ears are the largest of any elephant and shaped like the African continent. **Calf ears
  are proportionally smaller than adults' but grow fast** [San Diego Zoo]. Ours: ear height ≈ 0.6 × head
  height (stylized appeal: slightly larger).
* Tail: thin, ≈ 0.35 × shoulder height, ending in a tuft of coarse hair.
* Feet: round pads, three or four toenails visible on the front.

## Elephant skeleton → rig
Spine (4 bones: pelvis, lumbar, thorax, withers), neck (1 short, stiff), head, trunk (6-bone chain that
tapers), ears L/R (hinge at the front edge, so a flap rotates about a vertical axis), tail (3), legs (two
bones each, upper and lower, plus a foot). The forelimb elbow and the hind knee are nearly straight in
stance: elephants walk on columnar legs.

## Elephant gait table (walk)
Hutchinson et al. 2006 measured 2,400+ strides from 14 African and 48 Asian elephants [JEB 209:3812]:
* **Always a lateral-sequence footfall at every speed.** As speed rises it shifts toward a 25 % limb phase
  (singlefoot). **Never a whole-body aerial phase.** The hindquarters go briefly airborne only at
  Froude ≈ 1.
* Forelimb duty factor is greater than hindlimb duty factor.
* Smaller elephants use relatively higher stride frequency and longer relative strides than adults.
* Speeds measured ran from a 0.40 m/s slow walk up to 6.8 m/s.

| gait | speed (calf) | stride freq | β fore | β hind | phase LH LF RH RF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slow walk | 0.5 m/s | 0.55 Hz | 0.78 | 0.74 | 0.00 0.22 0.50 0.72 |
| walk | 1.0 m/s | 0.75 Hz | 0.70 | 0.66 | 0.00 0.24 0.50 0.74 |
| fast walk ("amble") | 1.8 m/s | 1.0 Hz | 0.58 | 0.52 | 0.00 0.25 0.50 0.75 |

Body motion: a heavy, slow weight shift. The pelvis rolls ±3° toward the stance hind leg. The head bobs
gently once per stride. Ears lag the head (follow-through). The trunk swings like a pendulum, lagging the
head by about 0.15 stride.

## Elephant behaviour beats (idle, acting)
* **Ear flapping** for cooling: blood vessels on the ear's inner side shed heat, and flapping moves air
  over them [SeaWorld; Cleveland Metroparks]. Idle flap: a slow 0.3–0.5 Hz fan while hot.
* Ears are also **signals**: flared wide makes the animal look bigger (unsure or scared); flapping can mean
  excitement or joy [Global Elephants].
* A calf's **trunk has little coordination at first**: it swings and flops, and the calf learns to use it
  over months [San Diego Zoo]. A comic, endearing trait for a young hero.
* Herd care by aunts and sisters ("allomothering") [Global Elephants]: a warm family motif.
* Tail swish 0.2–0.4 Hz. Weight shifts between the front feet when standing. Slow blinks.
