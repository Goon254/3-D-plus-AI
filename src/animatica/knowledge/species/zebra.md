# Plains zebra (young) — Equus quagga

## Zebra proportions
* Adults: shoulder height **127–140 cm**, head–body length 217–246 cm, tail 47–56.5 cm [Wikipedia, Plains
  zebra]. Our young zebra: 1.15 m at the shoulder. Body length ≈ 1.6 × shoulder height; legs ≈ 0.55 ×
  shoulder height.
* All zebras share a **large head, sturdy neck, long legs, a dorsal stripe, a tasselled tail and a
  bristly, erect mane**. The stripes run on up into the mane [Wikipedia].
* Plains zebra stripes are **broad: vertical on the forequarters, turning toward horizontal on the
  hindquarters** and legs.
* Ears are upright with rounded tips and turn in almost any direction. **Flattened-back ears signal
  trouble**; forward means interest [Wikipedia; San Diego Zoo].

## Zebra skeleton → rig
Spine (3), neck (3), head, jaw, ears L/R (two-axis), mane (rigid, follows the neck), tail (3 + tassel),
legs as an equid: fore = humerus (short), radius, cannon, hoof; hind = femur, tibia, cannon, hoof. The hock
points backward. For animation, 2 IK bones plus a foot per leg is enough.

## Zebra gait table
Equid gaits; zebras use the same walk/trot/canter/gallop set as horses [MDPI Animals 13:2557;
PMC10451235]:
* **Walk**: four-beat **lateral sequence** LH → LF → RH → RF, evenly spaced; always at least two feet down;
  **β ≈ 0.6–0.65**. The head and neck nod twice per stride (once per forefoot landing).
* **Trot**: two-beat **diagonal pairs** (LH + RF, then RH + LF) with a short suspension; **β ≈ 0.35–0.45**.
* **Canter**: asymmetric three-beat (outside hind; inside hind + outside fore together; inside fore), then
  suspension; β ≈ 0.3.

| gait | speed (young) | stride freq | β | phase LH LF RH RF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| walk | 1.4 m/s | 0.85 Hz | 0.62 | 0.00 0.25 0.50 0.75 |
| trot | 3.2 m/s | 1.35 Hz | 0.40 | 0.00 0.50 0.50 0.00 |
| canter (right lead) | 5.5 m/s | 1.6 Hz | 0.32 | 0.00 0.30 0.30 0.55 |

Body motion: walk: the withers dip on each fore landing, the head nods down 4–6° per fore landing, and the
tail swings. Trot: the whole body rises and falls twice per stride and the head stays fairly level. Canter:
a rocking-horse pitch, ±5°.

## Zebra behaviour beats
* Ears swivel independently toward sounds. Tail swishes at flies. Stamps a foot. Snorts.
* Mutual grooming with friends (nibbling the neck and withers) to build bonds [Wikipedia].
* Rolls in dust. Bolts at a sudden noise, then sheepishly comes back (comedy).
* A young zebra character: fast, proud of its speed, a bit of a show-off. Its arc is learning to wait for
  slower friends.
