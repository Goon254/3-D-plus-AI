# Leopard tortoise — Stigmochelys pardalis

## Tortoise proportions
* In East African savannahs it reaches **300–450 mm carapace length and ~13 kg**; exceptional animals
  exceed 40 kg and 750 mm [SANBI]. It is the savanna's largest tortoise after the African spurred
  tortoise.
* **High-domed shell**: extra protection from the sun's heat lets it stay out in open habitat longer
  [SANBI]. Pattern: dark spots and blotches on a cream-yellow shell ("pardalis" = spotted, as a leopard);
  the pattern fades with age.
* Ours (an elder): carapace 0.55 m long, 0.32 m high, 0.42 m wide. The head is ≈ 0.18 × carapace length
  on a neck that extends ≈ 0.15 m. The legs are thick columns with scaly front surfaces and elephant-like
  hind feet.

## Tortoise skeleton → rig
The shell is rigid (carapace + plastron = spine/root). Neck (3-bone chain: extends and retracts), head,
jaw, eyelids, legs: fore (upper, lower, foot) splayed out sideways, and hind (thigh, shin, foot) as
columns. Tail: tiny (1 bone).

## Tortoise gait table
Hermann's tortoise study [J. Exp. Biol. 219:2693 (2016)]: the slow overall walk comes from a **low stride
frequency**, with a **lateral-sequence, diagonally coupled footfall and high duty factors**. Its
shoulder-girdle rotation adds to step length. Cross-species work places reptiles in a high-duty group with
**β ≈ 0.8 and phase 0.4–0.5** (diagonal couplets), where mechanical work is minimised [Frontiers in Zoology
2019].

Body motion: the shell rocks and yaws slightly (±2°) toward the leg that is lifting. Each step lifts the
shell a little, then sets it back down. The head pushes forward with each fore step (counter-bob). Every
movement is slow and deliberate, with long holds.

## Tortoise behaviour beats
* Grazes: neck stretches toward the grass, then a slow crunch. Withdraws the head into the shell when
  startled, then peeks out (a comic or tender beat).
* Male shoving matches can overturn a rival [SANBI], so in our film no one gets flipped: the elder is
  calm.
* Lives 50–100 years. A **wise elder**: the slowest friend, who turns out to know the way, because slow and
  steady notices what fast animals miss.
