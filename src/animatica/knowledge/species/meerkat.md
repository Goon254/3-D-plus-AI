# Meerkat — Suricata suricatta

## Meerkat proportions
* Head + body **25–35 cm**; tail **17–25 cm**: thin and tapering, not bushy; weight 0.6–1 kg [National
  Geographic Kids; Loro Parque; EOL].
* Long, slim body; short legs. **Strong curved, non-retractable claws ~2 cm** on the forefeet for
  digging. **Crescent-shaped ears that close when digging** [Animal Corner; Cleveland Metroparks].
* Dark eye patches (natural "sunglasses") and a pointed snout. Silvery-brown fur with **irregular dark
  bands across the back and rump, unique to each animal**.
* Our meerkat: 0.32 m head+body, tail 0.21 m. **Slim**: the torso is ~2.8× as long as it is wide (0.235 ×
  0.084 m); at 2.2× (the first build) it walked like a striped hamster. Legs long enough to show daylight
  under the belly, a narrow head tapering into a pointed snout, eyes big for appeal.
* Markings: faint bands across the **top** of the back and rump only (they fade out down the flanks), and
  soft dark patches painted round the eyes, which stay visible inside them (solid black discs read as
  sunglasses).

## Meerkat skeleton → rig
Spine (4: it bends a lot, standing up and curling), neck (2), head, jaw, ears, arms L/R (upper, lower,
paw), legs L/R (thigh, shin, foot: plantigrade-ish), tail (4). **Two body modes**: quadruped (spine
horizontal) and **sentinel (spine vertical, sitting on the hind legs plus the tail as a tripod)**.

## Meerkat gait table
No peer-reviewed meerkat gait study was found. Observations: the walk is a *plodding* gait, with the
**hind foot placed just behind the forefoot before that forefoot lifts** [iNaturalist naturalist journal,
informal]. Small mustelid/herpestid mammals generally use a lateral-sequence walk at low speed and
**half-bound / bound** at speed (fore feet nearly together, hind feet together).

| gait | speed | stride freq | β | phase LH LF RH RF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| walk | 0.35 m/s | 2.0 Hz | 0.65 | 0.00 0.30 0.50 0.80 |
| scurry (half-bound) | 1.6 m/s | 4.0 Hz | 0.35 | 0.00 0.55 0.08 0.62 |

Body motion: walk: the head bobs and scans. Scurry: the spine flexes and extends once per stride (bound),
and the tail held out level for balance.

## Meerkat behaviour beats
* **Sentinel**: rises onto the hind legs, balancing on the legs plus the tail, to scan for danger. Group
  members take turns, and vigilance rises when pups are around [Cambridge study; Phys.org 2013]. It also
  stands up in the morning sun to warm its belly [New World Encyclopedia].
* Alarm call, then everyone dashes for the burrow. Head swivels in quick little jerks (saccades) with
  freezes in between.
* Digs with rapid foreclaw strokes, kicking sand back. Forages 5–8 hours a day, mostly insects
  [Animal Corner].
* Lives in groups of up to ~40. Very social, chattering. A meerkat character: a nervous, fast-talking
  lookout who thinks everything is danger (comedy), and is right once when it counts.
