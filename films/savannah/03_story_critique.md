# Story editor's notes on draft 1

Read as a story editor for a preschool and early-reader series, and as the production supervisor who has
to animate it. What's good, what fails, and the fix for each.

## What works (keep)
* **A clear want and a visible flaw.** Tembo wants to prove he's big enough; his wobbly trunk shows his
  insecurity in a way kids can *see*. The trunk spray bookends the film (the joke becomes the triumph).
* **A distinct ensemble.** Each friend has a different silhouette, speed and gift. The theme comes from
  the cast itself, not from a speech.
* **Mild, appropriate suspense.** Getting lost at dusk, no predators, nobody hurt. The scariest moment is
  a scared zebra calling "Hellooo?"
* **Jokes for two audiences.** Kids get the spray and Neema's head stuck in a tree. Parents get "Very
  refreshing" and "Nobody panic… EVERYBODY PANIC!" Also the wait-for-the-tortoise gag.
* **Time-of-day arc.** Dawn → midday → sunset → night → dawn mirrors the emotional arc and gives the
  visuals variety.

## Problems and fixes

| # | problem | why it matters | fix in draft 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **5:50 running time** against a 5:00 target | a kids' attention span; render budget | Merge SC3 + SC4 (the decision and the team form in one scene). Merge SC7 into SC6. Trim narration. Target ≈ 5:10. |
| 2 | **The hero doesn't drive the turn.** In Act 3 Bibi Kobe solves it (stars), and Kiki and Neema find it | a protagonist who watches others save the day is the classic weak-hero note | At the low point, **Tembo chooses** to stop and ask each friend what *they* notice, remembering his own line "You'd get lonely back here." He organizes the team, and the stars, the sound and the trees come from that. At the log, his growth is *asking for help*. |
| 3 | **The lesson is said three times** (Zuri SC8, narrator twice in SC11) | preachy; parents roll their eyes | Keep Zuri's line (it's her character truth). The narrator says the theme **once**, short. Cut "being big doesn't mean…": the trunk drink shared with Bibi Kobe *shows* it. |
| 4 | **Neema's arc isn't set up.** "Shy about her height" exists only in the cast table | the payoff (stretching tall to spot Zuri) lands with no setup | SC2: Neema **ducks and slouches** to look smaller. Zuri teases kindly ("lamp-post"). SC6: Tembo says "Neema, you're the tallest of all of us!" and she *stands all the way up* for the first time. |
| 5 | **Kiki's arc is flat.** False alarms, then she's simply right | there's no turn | After her false alarms, in SC8 she hears water but **doubts herself** ("What if it's just another rock?"). Tembo: "I believe you." Trust is her payoff. |
| 6 | **Names aren't introduced** before the characters act | kids lose track of five new animals | Name each friend aloud in SC2 (natural address: "Morning, Kiki!"). The narrator's line in SC3 introduces them by trait. |
| 7 | **"Tembo trots up"** | research: **elephants never trot or leave the ground** (Hutchinson 2006); they hurry with a fast walk | "Tembo *hurries* up, ears flapping." This is a motion note for the Animator too. |
| 8 | **Zuri "gallops" into the grass** | fine for a zebra, but our gait library needs a canter or gallop | keep, and **budget a canter** in the gait library (zebra: walk/trot/canter). |
| 9 | **The climax push is crowded** (five animals at one log) | staging and readability; hard to animate convincingly | Stage it in three clear beats: (a) Tembo alone fails; (b) "Then don't do it alone": Neema pulls a branch from above and Zuri pushes beside Tembo; (c) Kiki digs out the stone wedged under the log while Bibi Kobe calls the timing ("One… two… PUSH!"). One shot per beat. |
| 10 | **No breather in Act 2's middle** | fun-and-games, fork and loss are all action | a 3-second quiet beat in SC4: Tembo and Bibi Kobe walking, a butterfly on her shell. It sets up their bond for the low point. |
| 11 | **The final image doesn't close the trunk/drink motif** | — | Final shot: Tembo **sprays a small rainbow** in the dawn light, on purpose this time. Pure visual joy, no words. |
| 12 | **Producibility** | every action must map to the gait library + acting layers | Avoid physics (no rolling rocks; the log "rolls" as a keyed object). Kiki rides on Neema's head (a parent constraint). Keep crowd shots to at most five characters, and keep contact (pushing) to poses. |

## Kid-appropriateness check
* No death, no hunting, no injury. Zuri is scared but safe within 30 seconds of screen time.
* Nobody is mocked in a mean-spirited way: Zuri's teasing is friendly and she **apologises** in SC6.
* The lesson (everyone's gifts matter; wait for slower friends; ask for help) is right for ages 4–9.
* The music cue for being lost is soft and warm, not scary.

## Verdict
Draft 1 is a solid B. Do fixes 1–6 (structure, hero agency, setups and payoffs) before production; 7–12
are craft and production notes. Draft 2 follows.
